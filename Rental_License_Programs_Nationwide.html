Where these programs are preempted

State law forecloses or narrows local programs in several states — the map of what survives is itself instructive:

  • Indiana — Ind. Code 36-1-20-4.1 caps registration fees and restricts inspection programs; pre-1984 programs (Bloomington, West Lafayette) grandfathered.
  • Wisconsin — 2015 Act 176 and 2017 Act 317 sharply limit registration and inspection; Milwaukee’s program survives only in narrowed form.
  • North Carolina — G.S. 160D-1207 bars general registration/permits; only problem-property targeting allowed (ended Raleigh’s PROP).
  • Georgia — state law bars local registration of residential rental property.
  • Tennessee — no general ban. Davidson County’s registration is state-authorized by Tenn. Code Ann. § 66-28-107; a licensing or inspection program would need careful design against future preemption pushes. Austin’s problem-property model and fee-capped registries are the preemption-resilient designs.
