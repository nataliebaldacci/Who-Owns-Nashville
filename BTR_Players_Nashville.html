The Build-to-Rent Players in Nashville

Who Builds Nashville · Findings · built entirely from the Metro Nashville parcel roll

The Davidson County parcel roll names the build-to-rent operators on its own. Sort every parcel by the number held under one owner name, keep only owners who mail their tax bills outside Tennessee, and restrict the set to vacant and residential land. The build-to-rent operators rise to the top of the list.1

The Largest Out-of-State Residential Landholders

American Homes 4 Rent leads. Its development arm, AMH TN Development, LLC, holds 206 Davidson parcels and mails to Calabasas, California. Four more American Homes 4 Rent vehicles work the same Calabasas mailbox, and across every entity the company holds roughly 1,077 of these parcels.1 Behind it sit the national field of build-to-rent developers and their land bankers. Seven of the thirteen largest holders mail to just two out-of-state capitals: Scottsdale, Arizona and Calabasas, California. Not one of the ten largest residential landholders mails to a Tennessee address.

The Complete Roster

The table above ranks by raw parcel count, so it surfaces the operators building at the largest scale. The full field of build-to-rent players in Davidson County is wider. Several self-develop under smaller footprints, several buy new construction rather than hold land, and several sit one layer up as the development capital or land bank feeding a builder. The roster below names every operator identified in the parcel record and in SEC filings, grouped by role. Parcel counts come from the same Layer 4 query; national-operator activity comes from the filings cited.

Reading Parcel Ownership to Find Build-to-Rent Construction

Querying the largest out-of-state parcel owners in Davidson County reveals the county's ongoing build-to-rent construction.1 Sorting the Metro Nashville ownership layer by parcel count, filtered to owners outside Tennessee, surfaces institutional operators assembling houses under single corporate names rather than dispersed individual buyers.2 The top holders read as a build-to-rent roster: AVA MADISON, LLC (199 parcels), AMH MOUNTAIN SPRINGS DEVELOPMENT, LLC (186), CRESCENT BTR NASHVILLE SONYA DRIVE, LLC (173), and TPG AG EHC III MTH MULTI STATE 2, LLC (133), each recording to an out-of-state corporate address in Atlanta, Calabasas, Charlotte, or Scottsdale.3

Two fields in the query separate construction activity from finished holdings. A feature type of "Condo" carrying zero acres marks units created under a horizontal property regime, where the developer conveys each dwelling as a volume of space and holds the land as an undivided common element rather than a subdivided lot.4 A null land-use description on those same records marks parcels the assessor has not yet classified, the signature of units created but not yet complete.5 The leading holders show this pairing directly: Ava Madison, Crescent BTR, and TPG AG each hold Condo-feature-type parcels at zero acres with null land use, placing their projects mid-construction rather than stabilized.6

The pattern separates build-to-rent developers from operators buying scattered finished houses. A developer platting a horizontal regime consolidates an entire project under one master parcel and one grantee, producing the high per-owner parcel counts the query surfaces, while a scattered-site buyer accumulates individually described lots across the county.7 The zero-acre condo signature, paired with a null classification and an out-of-state capital address, identifies where institutional capital is building rental housing rather than merely holding it.1

A Case Study: Ava Madison, LLC

Ava Madison shows the mechanism. All 199 of its parcels carry the feature type "Condo," no land-use description, and Specific Plan zoning, and each reports zero acres.8 The developer recorded a master deed under Tennessee's Horizontal Property Act, which turns a single tract into a stack of individually deeded units that share the common ground. Metro's assessor then issues one parcel identifier per future unit. A subdivision built for rent enters the tax roll not as one tract but as 199 separate condominium parcels, each a home the operator will lease rather than sell.

The rezoning that authorized the project is a matter of public record. The Metro Council adopted BL2021-751, a Specific Plan, to permit the Ava Madison development.9 The permit trail names the same actor: a search of Metro's ePermits system for "Ava Madison" returns the building permits filed against the parcels, tied to the Atlanta entity at 3060 Peachtree Road, Suite 960.10

Detection recipe. Out-of-state owner + high parcel count under one name + feature type "Condo" at zero acres + null land use = a build-to-rent development under construction. Confirm any single parcel with its recorded master deed and its ePermits building permit.

The Legal Form: Horizontal Property Regime, Not Subdivision

Tennessee divides land among multiple owners through two mechanisms that produce different titles from the same ground. A subdivision splits a parent tract into separately described lots, each conveyed in fee simple with land running to the property line. A horizontal property regime instead carves the project into units and common elements, giving each buyer a defined volume of space plus an undivided interest in commonly held land.11 The buyer owns that volume, not the dirt beneath it, and takes easements across the surrounding land for access while the ground stays an undivided common element.12 To create the regime, a developer records a master deed, lease, or plat that enumerates the statutory particulars, and each sale then conveys a unit subject to that declaration rather than a lot carved from the tract.13

This structure yields a consequence subdivision cannot. The Tennessee Attorney General has opined that establishing a regime is not a subdivision, so a complying developer escapes the lot-size, frontage, and planning-approval requirements that subdivision triggers.14 Because the form fits detached houses as readily as stacked condominiums, a detached single-family house recorded under a regime carries the same volumetric title, and the same shared land, as a unit in a multistory building.15 That is why a build-to-rent operator can hold 199 homes on zero recorded acres.
