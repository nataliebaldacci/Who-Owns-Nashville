Build-to-rent

Five models of build-to-rent, and their impact

Every build-to-rent parcel in Davidson County sorts into one of five models by who builds and who holds. The categories decide the housing impact: whether a home is added to the stock or diverted from ownership, and whether it ever reaches a homebuyer at all.

The five models

Held (built, still owned by operator)Pipeline (being built)Exit (sold to a homebuyer)

The impact

Homes diverted from ownership. Of the 3,478 build-to-rent parcels, 3,227 (92.8 percent) are held or still in the pipeline, and only 251 (7.2 percent) ever exited to a homebuyer. A build-to-rent home is a home a family cannot buy.

Addition versus substitution. The vertically integrated model on greenfield land (about 691 parcels, led by American Homes 4 Rent) adds homes that likely would not otherwise exist. The one-off-sale model (216 parcels, VineBrook and Starwood) removes homes a builder had platted for sale. The partnership and contractor models sit between: most are new supply, but the home still reaches an operator rather than an owner.

The federal exemption. The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act would bar the acquisition channel that built the first institutional wave, but its build-to-rent exception (Section 1001(a)(2)(B)) reaches none of these 3,478 parcels. Every model here is exempt.

Value capture and yield. The pre-development land bank captures value before a foundation is laid: Ava Madison's 199 Tessa Madison lots gained a 4.2-times appraisal lift from rezoning and platting alone. On the built product, Nashville commands the highest per-foot rent in the operators' Southeast book at 1,826 dollars a month and 0.86 dollars per square foot.
