Owner- vs Renter-Occupied One-Unit Homes

CRS Figure 1, recreated — with Nashville–Davidson County added

The Congressional Research Service opens its 2026 report on institutional investors by noting that most single-unit homes are owner-occupied: about 84% nationally in 2024, little changed since 2010. Nashville looked the same in 2010 — and then diverged.

Owner-occupiedRenter-occupied
The divergence. In 2010, Davidson County's one-unit homes were 83.5% owner-occupied — essentially the national rate. By 2024 that had fallen to 80.3%, pushing the renter share of one-unit homes to 19.7%, about four points above the national ~16%.

Sources. National: Congressional Research Service, Institutional Investors and Single-Family Housing: In Brief, R49015 (June 30, 2026), Fig. 1, from U.S. Census Bureau ACS 1-Year, Table B25032 (2010 & 2024). Davidson County: U.S. Census Bureau ACS, Table B25032 (Tenure by Units in Structure), one-unit = detached + attached. Owner/renter of occupied one-unit properties.
