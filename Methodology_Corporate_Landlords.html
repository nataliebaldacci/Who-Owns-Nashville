Identifying Corporate Landlords in Nashville

Methodology · Davidson County · built entirely from primary government records

Large residential owners rarely hold property under one name. They register, buy, and evict through networks of single-purpose LLCs, borrower entities, and management companies designed to obscure the scale of common ownership. To measure that concentration, the individual shells have to be linked back to the operator that controls them. This page documents how we do that for Nashville's landlord-registration record.

1. Data sources (all primary, all replayable)

  • Metro ePermits landlord registrations — case type 176 (PSLANDLORD), joined to each case's address (APN), people (owners, managers, applicants), and fee ledger.
  • Metro ParcelHistory deed records — 1,641,434 ownership events (current owner, mailing address, acquisition date, recorded deed URL).
  • MASTER parcels crosswalk — APN ↔ internal ParID, for joining registrations to deeds and holdings.
  • Davidson Circuit Court (CaseLink) — detainer warrants, for the eviction layer.

2. Linking shells to operators

Our approach is a hybrid of the deductive and inductive strategies described by Shelton and Seymour (2024): start from a known operator and find its shells, and start from an unattributed shell and resolve who controls it. Each registered owner name passes through the following resolution chain.

  1. Name normalization. Upper-cased, punctuation stripped to spaces, space-padded, so L.L.C., L L C, and LLC collapse to one token and CO no longer matches "COURT."
  2. Entity flag. A name is treated as a business entity if it carries a legal-form token (LLC, LP, INC, LTD, CORP, COMPANY, ASSOCIATION, and so on).
  3. Shell → parent resolution, in order of confidence:
    • Known-shell patterns — deterministic name rules for documented shell families (for example, MCH SFR→ My Community Homes; AH4R→ American Homes 4 Rent).
    • Security-for-Sale crosswalk — the McClatchy entity→parent table, matched on normalized name, for national operators.
    • Canonical owner→operator crosswalk — a curated table built from Nashville's current cadastral holdings, mapping each owner name to its operator and ultimate parent.
    • Operator name-pattern rules — explicit, auditable filters for operators whose shells share a naming series or a registered address. Example: Invitation Homes is matched on its borrower series (SFR XII, SFR CB, SN TENNESSEE, SWH, CSH, INVITATION HOMES) together with its corporate address at 3630 Peachtree Road, Suite 500, Atlanta.
  4. Address clustering. Registrations are grouped by owner mailing address and ZIP to surface shells that share a single mailbox — the highest-yield linkage step in Shelton and Seymour's account. Counting the distinct shells behind one address is itself a measure of ownership fragmentation.
  5. State registry verification. Entities are matched against the Tennessee Secretary of State business-entity registry (tncab.tnsos.gov) to pull each entity's control number, status, registered agent, and principal office address. Tennessee publishes no bulk officer data, so the principal office address becomes the linking bridge: shells that resolve to the same principal office or registered agent are attributed to the same operator — a more authoritative link than a mailing address printed on a deed.
  6. Officer layer. Every registration's associated people (owners, managers, applicants) are captured, preserving the raw material for principal/agent network linkage.
What this method is, and is not. This is a curated-crosswalk and rule-based resolution, cross-checked against current holdings and validated case by case. It is not an automated name-similarity or officer-network community-detection pipeline of the kind implemented in Hangen and O'Brien (2024) and the MIT Spatial Action Lab's whoownsmass-processing project. We draw on that literature and share its goal; we do not run its clustering code. Where the rule-based approach and the holdings record disagree, the disagreement is resolved by hand and recorded.

3. Validation and corrections

  • Shared tax-agent mailboxes are not, by themselves, an ownership signal. A single Scottsdale post-office box (care of a tax-agent firm) is used by several unrelated single-family operators, so address alone is not sufficient to merge entities there.
  • Securitization names are checked against the sponsor, not the servicer. The STAR 20XX-SFR borrower series is Starwood Capital's own program and is attributed to Starwood, not to Invitation Homes.
  • Servicers are kept distinct from principals. RESICAP, an independent single-family servicer/operator, is not folded into Invitation Homes. Corporate relocation companies (which hold homes only transiently) are excluded from operator counts entirely.

Current owners and historical owners: two datasets

Corporate ownership is measured two ways, from two primary datasets, because “who owns it now” and “who has ever owned it” are different questions.

Current owners — the parcel framework

The current cadastral (Ownership Parcels, MapServer/4) carries one row per parcel with today’s owner, mailing address, and state. This is the basis of the research framework and scope: the 205,933 one- and two-unit homes that fit the 21st Century Housing Act, filtered to the corporate owners holding them right now.

Dataset → Ownership Parcels, MapServer/4 ↗ · built into the Data Filter Funnel and Research Scope.

Historical owners — the transaction history

The current cadastral shows only the present owner, so it cannot say who held a home as an investor five or ten years ago. For that we read the Ownership History (ParcelHistory, MapServer/2): 1,641,434 recorded deed events, one per transfer, each carrying the owner name, mailing address, and state at the time, the acquisition date, and the recorded instrument. Running the same corporate test over the deed record surfaces every entity that ever took title, not just the current holder.

Dataset → Ownership History, MapServer/2 — run the corporate-owner deed query ↗

A recorded owner is treated as a corporate entity when its name carries a business token and is not a government body or a family trust:

(Name LIKE '%LLC%' OR '%LLP%' OR '% LP%' OR '%L.P%' OR '% INC%' OR '%CORP%' OR '%LTD%' OR '%LIMITED%' OR '% CO %' OR '%CO.%' OR '%COMPANY%' OR '%PARTNER%' OR '%ASSOC%' OR '%HOMES%' OR '%HOLDING%' OR '%CAPITAL%' OR '%INVEST%' OR '%FUND%' OR '%SOLUTION%' OR '%SFR%' OR '%ASSET%' OR '%BORROW%' OR '% JV%') minus government (CITY OF, HOUSING AUTH, METRO...GOV, STATE OF, COUNTY OF, HUD, MDHA, UNITED STATES) minus trusts (TRUST, REVOCABLE, LIVING TR, FAMILY TR)

Out of state is StateCode <> 'TN'. Out of county is a mailing ZIP outside the Davidson County set (37013, 37072, 37076, 37080, 37115, 37138, 37189, and 37201–37221, 37228). Grouping the corporate deed events by owner name and keeping those with more than a couple of transfers ranks operators by how active they have been across the entire history, not just their current book.

Owner-type classification and deduplication

Before any owner-level count, every recorded owner name is normalized, deduplicated, clustered, and classified. The full transaction record (1,641,434 deed events, all instrument types, 1969–2026) carries these fields.

Name and address cleaning

The owner name is upper-cased, punctuation stripped, and legal-form tokens collapsed to one spelling, so L.L.C., L L C, and LLC become a single token. The mailing address is normalized the same way, with street-suffix and PO-box standardization. Cleaning cuts 934,939 raw names to 911,940 and 486,208 raw addresses to 467,819.

Owner clustering and parent rollup

A Union-Find pass merges entities that share a normalized mailing address. It is restricted to business entities and skips any address used by more than five distinct names, so shared tax-agent mailboxes and apartment buildings do not merge unrelated owners; any component larger than 150 names is broken, so no hairball forms. This yields 899,639 clusters. A parent-brand rollup then folds each operator’s naming variants into one owner: FOX RIDGE HOMES, FOX RIDGE CO, and NVR SAVINGS BANK all resolve to NVR / Ryan Homes. The result is 896,642 final owners across 467,819 mailing addresses.

Owner type

Each name is classified in priority order, first match wins: Government, then Bank or Lender, then Corporate, then Trust, then Individual. Priority resolves the hard cases. A name carrying a corporate token is Corporate before it can be read as a trust, so OPENDOOR PROPERTY TRUST and ZILLOW HOMES PROPERTY TRUST are Corporate rather than personal trusts, and AMERICAN HOMES 4 RENT TRS, LLC is Corporate rather than a trustee record. The trust test uses a word boundary on TRUST, so a person recorded as TRUSTEE stays out.

Result across 1,641,434 deed events. Individual 1,194,589 deeds (844,084 owners) · Corporate 355,221 (62,114) · Trust 32,262 (22,775) · Bank or Lender 27,531 (3,696) · Government 31,831 (2,270). Keyword rules: Corporate = LLC, LP, INC, CORP, HOMES, PROPERT, HOLDING, GROUP, PARTNER, INVEST, CAPITAL, SFR, ASSET, BORROW, and peers; Trust = TRUST, REVOCABLE, IRREVOCABLE (word-boundary); Bank/Lender = BANK, MORTG, FINANCIAL, LEND, CREDIT, LOAN; Government = SEC OF, FEDERAL, METRO GOV, STATE OF, HUD, MDHA, THDA, and peers. Full keyword lists

Every token, checked in priority order. Short tokens are matched on a word boundary so they do not hit inside surnames.

  1. 1. Government — SEC OF, SECRETARY OF, HUD, VETERAN, FANNIE, FREDDIE, FEDERAL, METRO GOV, GOV’T, GOVERNMENT, STATE OF, UNITED STATES OF, POSTAL SERVICE, CITY OF, COUNTY OF, HOUSING AUTH, AGENCY, MDHA (and M.D.H.A / M D H A), THDA (and T.H.D.A / T H D A)
  2. 2. Bank / Lender — BANK, MORTG, FINANCIAL, SAVINGS, LEND / LENDER, CREDIT, LOAN, NATIONAL ASSOC, VELOCITY RES, N.A
  3. 3. Corporate — LLC, LLP, LP, INC, CORP, LTD, COMPANY, CO, PARTNER, ASSOC, HOLDING, HOMES, PROPERT, GROUP, CAPITAL, INVEST, FUND, DEVELOP, BUILDER, REALTY, SFR, ASSET, BORROW, ACQUISITION, BUYER, CONSTRUCTION, VENTURE, ENTERPRISE
  4. 4. Trust — TRUST (word-boundary, so TRUSTEE is excluded), REVOCABLE, IRREVOCABLE. Nothing shorter: TR, TR., TRS, and standalone FAMILY / LIVING are not used, because they mark a trustee role or catch SINGLE FAMILY / HIGHLAND-style false positives.
  5. 5. Individual — everything that matches none of the above (about 91% of owners). Corroborating markers: ETUX / ET UX, JR / SR, the ampersand for spousal owners, and the assessor’s LAST, FIRST comma format.
Why not the bare tokens. The live server uses LIKE, a raw substring, so short tokens leak: %LOAN% catches SLOAN, %LEND% catches GLENDA, %GOV% catches MCGOVERN, and %TRUST% catches the corporate PROPERTY TRUST. On the server these are guarded with spaces (% LOAN%) and priority exclusions; on the cleaned dataset they are matched on word boundaries.
