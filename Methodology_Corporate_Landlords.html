Identifying Corporate Landlords in Nashville

Methodology · Davidson County · built entirely from primary government records

Large residential owners rarely hold property under one name. They register, buy, and evict through networks of single-purpose LLCs, borrower entities, and management companies designed to obscure the scale of common ownership. To measure that concentration, the individual shells have to be linked back to the operator that controls them. This page documents how we do that for Nashville's landlord-registration record.

1. Data sources (all primary, all replayable)

  • Metro ePermits landlord registrations — case type 176 (PSLANDLORD), joined to each case's address (APN), people (owners, managers, applicants), and fee ledger.
  • Metro ParcelHistory deed records — 1,641,434 ownership events (current owner, mailing address, acquisition date, recorded deed URL).
  • MASTER parcels crosswalk — APN ↔ internal ParID, for joining registrations to deeds and holdings.
  • Davidson Circuit Court (CaseLink) — detainer warrants, for the eviction layer.

2. Linking shells to operators

Our approach is a hybrid of the deductive and inductive strategies described by Shelton and Seymour (2024): start from a known operator and find its shells, and start from an unattributed shell and resolve who controls it. Each registered owner name passes through the following resolution chain.

  1. Name normalization. Upper-cased, punctuation stripped to spaces, space-padded, so L.L.C., L L C, and LLC collapse to one token and CO no longer matches "COURT."
  2. Entity flag. A name is treated as a business entity if it carries a legal-form token (LLC, LP, INC, LTD, CORP, COMPANY, ASSOCIATION, and so on).
  3. Shell → parent resolution, in order of confidence:
    • Known-shell patterns — deterministic name rules for documented shell families (for example, MCH SFR→ My Community Homes; AH4R→ American Homes 4 Rent).
    • Security-for-Sale crosswalk — the McClatchy entity→parent table, matched on normalized name, for national operators.
    • Canonical owner→operator crosswalk — a curated table built from Nashville's current cadastral holdings, mapping each owner name to its operator and ultimate parent.
    • Operator name-pattern rules — explicit, auditable filters for operators whose shells share a naming series or a registered address. Example: Invitation Homes is matched on its borrower series (SFR XII, SFR CB, SN TENNESSEE, SWH, CSH, INVITATION HOMES) together with its corporate address at 3630 Peachtree Road, Suite 500, Atlanta.
  4. Address clustering. Registrations are grouped by owner mailing address and ZIP to surface shells that share a single mailbox — the highest-yield linkage step in Shelton and Seymour's account. Counting the distinct shells behind one address is itself a measure of ownership fragmentation.
  5. Officer layer. Every registration's associated people (owners, managers, applicants) are captured, preserving the raw material for principal/agent network linkage.
What this method is, and is not. This is a curated-crosswalk and rule-based resolution, cross-checked against current holdings and validated case by case. It is not an automated name-similarity or officer-network community-detection pipeline of the kind implemented in Hangen and O'Brien (2024) and the MIT Spatial Action Lab's whoownsmass-processing project. We draw on that literature and share its goal; we do not run its clustering code. Where the rule-based approach and the holdings record disagree, the disagreement is resolved by hand and recorded.

3. Validation and corrections

  • Shared tax-agent mailboxes are not, by themselves, an ownership signal. A single Scottsdale post-office box (care of a tax-agent firm) is used by several unrelated single-family operators, so address alone is not sufficient to merge entities there.
  • Securitization names are checked against the sponsor, not the servicer. The STAR 20XX-SFR borrower series is Starwood Capital's own program and is attributed to Starwood, not to Invitation Homes.
  • Servicers are kept distinct from principals. RESICAP, an independent single-family servicer/operator, is not folded into Invitation Homes. Corporate relocation companies (which hold homes only transiently) are excluded from operator counts entirely.
