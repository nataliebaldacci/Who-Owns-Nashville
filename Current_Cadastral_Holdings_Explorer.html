How we identified the owners

Corporate ownership in Nashville hides behind hundreds of separate LLCs, limited partnerships, and securitization trusts. A single operator can hold homes under dozens of entity names. To see the operator behind the shells, we build an ownership network from primary government records, following the "horizontal holdings" network approach of Shelton and Seymour (2024).

  1. Start from the primary record. Every parcel's legal owner name and owner mailing address comes from the Metro Nashville assessor's cadastral (Ownership Parcels layer). No third-party or proprietary ownership data is used, every parcel traces to a live county record.
  2. Flag corporate owners. A parcel is flagged corporate when the owner name carries a business token, LLC, L.L.C, LLP, LP, L.P, INC, CORP, LTD, LIMITED, CO., COMPANY, PARTNERS, ASSOC, HOMES, HOLDINGS, CAPITAL, INVEST, FUND, SOLUTIONS, SFR, ASSET, BORROW, or JV, and we exclude lenders and servicers (MORTGAGE, LOAN, SERVICING, LENDING, BANK) so mortgage holders are not miscounted as owners. This name-keyword step follows Redfin's investor-identification method and the business-keyword rule of Kestelman et al. (2026). We deliberately do not flag "TRUST," which sweeps in ordinary family and estate trusts.
  3. Normalize entity names. We uppercase, strip punctuation and extra whitespace, and standardize legal suffixes so spelling variants of the same entity collapse together.
  4. Link entities into operators. A union-find algorithm joins entities that share (a) a normalized owner name, (b) a common owner mailing address, or (c) a shared registered agent or principal-office address in the Tennessee Secretary of State business registry. Each connected component is one owner network, the local analogue of the "Final Owner Networks" of Kestelman et al. (2026).
  5. Resolve to the ultimate parent. Shell families are mapped to their parent brand (for example, YAMASA Co. and PR Borrower entities to Progress Residential; STAR 20XX-SFR borrowers to Starwood Capital; SFR JV borrowers to Tricon) using SEC filings (ABS-15G, 10-K, Schedule III), securitization deal names, and mailing-address convergence.
  6. Validate. We keep only currently-active ownership, drop shared tax-agent mailboxes that host multiple unrelated operators, and spot-check every cluster against live county records.
Yes, we use the Tennessee business data. Because Tennessee does not publish beneficial owners, the Tennessee Secretary of State business registry (pulled from TNBear) is the bridge that links otherwise-anonymous shells to one operator: entities filed by the same registered agent or listing the same principal-office address are joined into a single owner. tncab.tnsos.gov ↗

Where this method sits in the literature

Kestelman et al. (2026) set the current benchmark for measuring rental ownership: trace each property through its chain of entities to a final owner rather than aggregating by mailing address alone, which they show both under- and over-states portfolio size. Their national implementation relies on restricted federal microdata (the Census Bureau Business Register and IRS Schedule K-1 filings). We follow the same entity-resolution principle using public data, precisely the state-corporate-registry approach they describe, which Torres (2024) applies in Florida and An et al. (2024) in Georgia, here implemented for Tennessee through the Secretary of State registry. We heed their central caveat by never relying on mailing address alone, and we sort owners into the same economically meaningful categories they use: individuals, incorporated individuals, business entities, and public owners.
