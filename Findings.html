What the Record Shows

A written analysis of the findings across the whole series · every number links to the map or table that carries it · July 2026

This project set out to answer one question from primary government records alone: who owns Nashville's single-family housing, and what are they doing with it? The answer runs through five parts — who owns, who finances, who builds, who landbanks, and who can afford the result. Each finding below is drawn from the county's own parcel roll, deed records, permit system, SEC filings, and HUD administrative data, and links to the page where it can be checked.

1. Ownership is modest countywide — and extreme where it lands

The single most important correction this data makes to the public conversation is about scale and location at once. Countywide, the named national operators — Progress Residential, American Homes 4 Rent, Amherst, Tricon, Invitation Homes, FirstKey, Starwood and their peers — hold 3,940 of the county's 206,000 framework homes: 1.9%. Quoted alone, that number says the phenomenon is small. The geography says otherwise. Institutional share climbs past 13% of all homes in the southeast tracts, past 20% in a band of Antioch and Hermitage block groups, and reaches 70% of one block group and 100% of individual subdivisions — whole streets where every house has one landlord. By community, Antioch leads at 14.8% (driven by Progress) and Hermitage at 9.1% (driven by AMH). The countywide average and the neighborhood reality are different facts, and policy aimed at one will miss the other.

Attribution is the work behind that number. On paper the holdings belong to hundreds of shell LLCs — Yamasa Co., ARMM Asset Company 2, SFR JV-1 Borrower. Rolling them up through shared registered addresses, TN Secretary of State filings, landlord-registration records, and deed history — the same linkage method used by the Atlanta and Boston studies — consolidates them to parents: Progress 1,215 · AMH 1,182 · Amherst 699 · Regent 642 at the top of the corrected leaderboard. The owner directory exposes the whole graph, and the shared mail hubs tie the same parents to the Atlanta market: 5001 Plaza on the Lake (Pretium/Progress), 15771 Red Hill Ave (Tricon), 16220 N Scottsdale Rd (Invitation) anchor holdings in both cities.

2. The homes are collateral: Wall Street's paper trail runs through Davidson deeds

The rental houses are not just owned — they are pledged. 118 securitization deals put 1,833 Davidson homes up as bond collateral, each one traceable from the SEC exhibit that disclosed it to the quitclaim deed and deed of trust recorded at the county (the document trail). Nationally the same deals pledge 4,879 homes across five states. Two patterns stand out in the deal records. First, appreciation accrues to the bondholder structure: ARP 2014-1's Davidson collateral was appraised at $7.7M at closing and $17.4M now (+128%). Second, the homes stay put — median six years held and counting — because the rent stream is the asset: the median securitized home sits in a $2,390/month 3BR fair-market-rent area. The deed trail also records the machinery churning: deeds of trust, assignments, and modifications filed per deal, all linked from each deal's transaction table.

3. Build-to-rent is now the front door — and it mostly adds, with one exception

The newer channel is homes built to be rentals from the first nail. Sorted by the Urban Institute's test — who owns the land during construction — Davidson's 3,317 BTR parcels split into vertically integrated operators (753), investors hiring contract builders (1,741), builder-investor presale partnerships (717), and one-off bulk sales of unsold for-sale homes (106). Three of the four models add supply that would not otherwise exist; only the fourth substitutes for homes that were headed to buyers. The flow-through confirms it: of every BTR parcel tracked, 92.8% is held or in the pipeline and 7.2% ever reached a homebuyer — and none of it is barred by the ROAD Act, which regulates the resale channel, not construction.

The never-occupied data makes the same point from the deed side: of 1,992 homes built since 2010 that no owner ever occupied, 636 sit with a named national operator — and 577 of those 636 are AMH's, the signature of the one large operator that builds and holds at scale here. The legal vehicle matters too: BTR communities are typically recorded as horizontal property regimes, not subdivisions, which lets an operator hold 199 homes on zero recorded acres and, per the Tennessee Attorney General, escape subdivision review entirely. Two local builders — Elmington Residential and Ole South Properties — supply most of the pipeline across several competing operators (community crosswalk).

4. The pipeline starts with land, held by out-of-state capital

Upstream of construction, the land itself is banked. Not one of the ten largest out-of-state residential landholders in the county mails its tax bill to a Tennessee address; seven of the top thirteen mail to just Scottsdale, Arizona or Calabasas, California (the players). Dedicated land-bank vehicles — TPG/Angelo Gordon (169 parcels), Kennedy Lewis (131), Domain, Walton Global's 211 raw acres — hold platted lots for builder clients under option agreements recorded at the Register of Deeds (vacant land, Walton tracts, land over time). The mailing-address map generalizes the point: absentee control of Davidson parcels concentrates in a handful of corporate HQ cities, and 17,093 existing homes have been bought arms-length by out-of-state owners since 2000, with an unmistakable surge in 2021.

5. What it costs: rents outran incomes, and the bottom fell out

The demand-side backdrop, from HUD's own administrative series: the Nashville two-bedroom Fair Market Rent rose 80% from 2017 to 2026 ($959 → $1,730) while median family income rose 69% ($68,700 → $116,100). At the bottom of the distribution the arithmetic no longer closes: a four-person extremely-low-income household (the 30%-of-median limit, $34,850 in 2026) can afford $871 a month at the 30% standard — $859 short of the FMR, which would consume 60% of its income. And the county is not one rental market: 2026 two-bedroom rents run from $970 to $2,600 across its ZIP codes, a 2.7× spread that maps directly onto the ownership geography (rent vs. income).

6. The through-line: one geography carries every layer

Lay the layers over each other and the same southeast crescent — Antioch, Cane Ridge, Hermitage, the corridor along I-24 — lights up in all of them: the highest institutional share, the BTR communities, the securitized collateral, the never-occupied new construction, the out-of-state buyer surge, and the property-standards violations recorded against institutional landlords. This is where new, relatively affordable single-family supply was built — and precisely because it was new, uniform, and cheap to manage, it is where every stage of the pipeline concentrated. The map of who owns Nashville is, at bottom, a map of where Nashville built starter homes.

The one-sentence version: institutional ownership of Nashville's houses is small countywide and severe in the places it targets; the homes are financed as bond collateral, increasingly built as rentals from the ground up on land banked by out-of-state capital, held through legal forms that avoid subdivision review, and rented into a market where the cheapest official rent now exceeds what a low-income family can pay.

Method & caveats

WHAT THIS IS BUILT FROM. Metro Nashville Cadastral and ParcelHistory ArcGIS layers, the ePermits OData API, Davidson County Register of Deeds images, TN Secretary of State filings, Metro landlord-registration records, SEC EDGAR exhibits, HUD User FMR/Income-Limits API, and ACS 2020–2024. Owner consolidation follows the conservative record-linkage used in the Atlanta (Who Owns Atlanta) and Boston (Hangen & O'Brien 2024) studies: name stems, TF-IDF similarity, shared non-agent addresses with hub exclusions, and manual review queues; ambiguous merges are flagged, not forced.
WHAT TO KEEP IN MIND. The 1.9% institutional share counts only the identified 350+‑home national operators; adding local investor LLCs raises corporate ownership to 8.1%, and the right figure depends on the question. Counts are snapshots (framework file July 2026; live-county queries dated on each page). The never-occupied figure (1,992) is this repo's reproduction of an analysis first run against the live parcel service (2,405) — the method note on that map states both. Owner-occupancy is a mailing-address proxy. A handful of crosswalk entries remain marked [verify] pending one more record.
