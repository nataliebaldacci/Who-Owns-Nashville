The series

Who Owns Nashville? — the aims of the series

A five-part study of institutional ownership of housing in one American county, built entirely from primary government records. Each part asks one question and follows the home from the property record to the person who lives in it.

Overarching aim

Build the most thorough parcel-level accounting of institutional housing activity in a single American county, using exclusively primary county-sourced data, and show that institutional ownership reaches far beyond the named REITs that receive all the scrutiny.

Introduction. AMH's "How can we help?" email after the March 2020 Nashville tornado: how a landlord became a first responder, and why the property record is the place to start.

PART V

Who Saves

What can be done?
Propose model legislation and transparency reforms calibrated to the evidence in Parts I–IV.
Six policy options, from registration audit to beneficial-ownership disclosure.
Conclusion. What parcel-level, primary-source transparency reveals that aggregate national estimates miss, and a replicable method any county can run.

The six ambitions

  1. The most thorough parcel-level accounting of institutional housing in a single county, from primary data alone.
  2. Show institutional ownership extends far beyond REITs (REITs are 18% of entity-owned housing; the other 82% goes unscrutinized).
  3. Reframe the geographic and demographic understanding of where institutional ownership concentrates.
  4. Connect ownership to construction through the builder-to-owner pipeline.
  5. Document what concentration means for the people who live in these homes: racial equity, cost burden, eviction, climate risk.
  6. Provide a replicable methodology, every claim a live, replayable government query.
